Layout Review

The race screen today, and a proposal that stays inside the Spectrum's rules. Numbers on the left screen match the findings below. The right screen is built from a real game screen, cell by cell, so everything in it can be drawn by the game.

The rules every change keeps to

  • One ink and one paper colour per 8 × 8 cell, and BRIGHT per cell. Every coloured panel starts and ends on a cell edge.
  • Anything that moves stays in the play window, which is one colour pair (blue on cyan). Nothing there can clash, because no cell ever needs a second ink.
  • Panels that sit next to each other use different papers, or one is BRIGHT and one isn't, so their edges read without lines.
  • The border is free: changing its colour costs no pixels and can't clash.
1

The message bar is empty while you race change

After GO, the two rows under the track's name go blank, while your place and lap are only tiny digits in the race order. Put them here, large: your place in bright white on red (your colour) and the lap in black on bright yellow, with the race time beside them.

Cost: drawn only when the place or lap changes - no speed cost while racing.

2

The play window: one colour pair keep

Blue on cyan with no white is right: it can't clash and it doesn't flicker. Cars are told apart by their shapes, and their race numbers, not by colour. Colouring them would bleed into the cloth around every car.

3

The race order is crowded while racing change

Each row squeezes a gap and a lap digit into the small font. With your lap at the top (1), drop the lap digit from every row and give the gap the room. Keep your row red, so you find yourself in a glance.

4

The mini-map keep

It reads well, and the dots move by XOR, so the map never has to be redrawn. The lap record sits above it, centred.

5

Your face is too blocky change

It is your 16 × 16 drawing at double size, so every pixel is a 2 × 2 block. Shown at its own size, in a 2 × 2 cell red panel, it matches the faces in the race order and leaves space for the bubble.

6

The crew chief, the same change

The same fix: 16 × 16 in a 2 × 2 cell blue panel, with the bubble's tail pointing at him.

7

The speech bubble fits its cells change

The bubble's white paper must fill whole cells, or a white band shows above or below it. The proposal sizes the bubble to rows 20-22 exactly and widens it to fill columns 11-20, so no white is left beside it; its tail is a white point in the crew chief's blue panel.

8

Use the border as a signal your call

Flash it green at GO, red when you crash and white when the NOS fires. It costs nothing and can't clash. (It shares a port with the speaker, and the sound code already keeps the border's colour, so this is a few bytes.)

One more row at the top of the play window?

Possible on memory now, but about 3% slower. The window would go from 176 × 120 to 176 × 128 pixels. Measured costs per pixel line, from the lap profile:

Total218.4k T+6.5k T (budget: 209.7k)

Memory: the screen-copy code grows by about 320 bytes into the top of memory. The big cars freed about 870 bytes in the main area, so the map rows kept up there can move down to make room.

Layout: the window's frame moves up into the message bar's second row, so the bar keeps one row: the big place and lap in (1) would become normal-size text. The game is already a little over budget in corners, since the new grip model, so the extra row is best done together with a speed pass.
